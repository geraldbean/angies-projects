Pipe Flow Lab

for fluid mechanics

Project Overview

From June 2026 to August 2026 I worked as an Assistant Course Developer at Queen’s University for courses in Fluid Mechanics and Thermodynamics. This pipe flow lab was a lab that I designed for a second year engineering Fluid Mechanics course (MREN 241). The goal was to create a lab that would allow students to explore Bernoulli’s equation plus losses by verifying flow rates, velocity changes, and pressure drops across different segments of a piping system.

The Mechanical Component


The lab was set up on a rectangular pegboard with an air blower, pitot tube and various lengths and diameters of PVC piping. In order to properly secure the pipes to the pegboard I designed 3D printed pipe fasteners to clip around each pipe and through the pegboard. A vertical clip and horizontal clip were both designed and each came in either a 1 inch or ¾ inch diameter to fit both PVC pipe sizes used in the lab.

The Electrical Component


In order for students to measure pressure drops across different losses in the piping system, I configured a pressure sensor circuit that would give an average pressure drop in inches of water. This circuit consisted of an Arduino Uno microcontroller, OLED screen, differential pressure sensors, and operational amplifiers. Each pressure sensor was connected to a rubber hose which then was connected to a pressure tap in the PVC piping. Each pressure sensor also required an operational amplifier with a gain of 750 ohms to amplify the voltage readings enough to observe a pressure drop. Each individual sensor’s data was processed using an Arduino microcontroller, and the average pressure drop was displayed on the OLED screen. Additionally, the Arduino average value function was used to level out the fluctuating pressure drop readings to be displayed for students during the lab.
